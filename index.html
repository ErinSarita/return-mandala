The Return Mandala

A map for returning to your own earliest story, read from the center out. At the center is the child you were, from your conception through your birth and your first hour. Around that child is you, now: the adult who can hold them. Around you is your circle of support, and around them the guide, who names the possible imprints of each month, the question the child you were may have begun to ask, and how it can sound in you today. On the outer petals are practices for meeting and healing the child you were. A companion to The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, turned around: here the child has grown up, and the work is yours.

About this mandala

A mandala is a circle organized around a center. This one is a companion to The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, which keeps the baby at the center so that a mother, a partner, and an educator can welcome her well. Here the same map is turned around. The baby has grown up. You may be thinking about becoming a parent, or not; you may have noticed patterns in yourself that seem older than your memory. Either way, the work of this wheel is yours: to return to your own beginning, understand it, and meet the child you were with the care that every child deserves.

How it is built

The circle begins at the top, with the first day of your mother's last period, and moves clockwise. Three quarters are the trimesters, three months each. The last quarter is your birth: labor, the birth itself, and your first hour. The months line up with those of The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, so the two can be laid side by side.

The rings line up month by month, so reading straight out from any slice shows, at the same moment, the child you were, how it may echo in you now, how your circle can hold you, and what a guide would bring up. The practices on the outer petals are gathered by quarter.

The rings are also layers of support: each holds the one inside it. You hold the child you were. Your circle holds you. Their own support holds them. Castellino taught that no one should hold it all alone, and that is as true of healing as of birth (Castellino, in White, 2013).

Around the petals, the time wheel holds every marker of time. Enter your birthday and it marks the full and new moons of your own time in the womb, the moon you were born under, and, if you know them, your due date and the details of your birth.

Attachment

The Attachment tab sets the four adult attachment styles beside the wheel, with the months the field links to each. Attachment research is among the most measured in psychology: the first years of care shape how a person expects closeness to go. The links to particular months of pregnancy and birth are the field's view, offered as possible early roots rather than findings. Most hopeful of all is earned security: adults who make sense of a hard start can become secure.

Going gently

This is reflective work, and it can stir strong feelings. Go one month at a time, and stop whenever you need to. The Pause at the top of the page is always there. You do not need to know the facts of your beginning to begin: many people cannot ask their parents, or should not, and the story you have is enough. If what surfaces is heavy, such as trauma, loss, or abuse, do this work alongside a trauma-informed therapist rather than alone. This mandala is for reflection and education. It is not therapy, and it does not diagnose.

  • Pace yourself. One month, one question, one practice. Castellino's Pause applies here: slowing down is part of the work, not a delay.
  • Hold your parents with compassion, and yourself too. Naming what happened is not blame. Most parents did what they could with what they had, and what you felt was still real.
  • Rupture and repair. What matters most after a hard moment is the return. That is as true between you and the child you were as between a parent and a baby (Strange).
  • Make meaning. Telling your story and being heard helps a hard beginning settle into one you can understand. If distress lingers, such as flashbacks, numbness, or avoidance, reach for trauma-informed support (Seng and Taylor).

Your own moons

Many traditions count a pregnancy as ten moons, and the forty weeks span nine or ten full moons. Entering your birthday marks the real full and new moons of the months you were carried, and names the phase of the moon on the day you were born. Tap or hover over a moon to see its date and how far along you were.

A note on language

The lines on what you may have sensed, and the possible imprints, are written with care. They draw on research and on the experience of the field's pioneers, and they describe possibilities, not certainties. Every person and every beginning is its own. Your mother here means the person who carried you; if you were adopted or born through surrogacy, that is your birth or gestational mother, and the people who raised you belong to the later chapters of your story, which matter just as much.
