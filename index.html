About this mandala

A mandala is a circle organized around a center. This one is a companion to The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, which keeps the baby at the center so that a mother, a partner, and an educator can welcome her well. Here the same center is turned around. The baby has grown up. You may be thinking about becoming a parent, or not; you may have noticed patterns in yourself that seem older than your memory. Either way, the work of this wheel is yours: to return to your beginning and to the passages of growing up, understand what they left, and meet the child you were with the care that every child deserves.

How it is built

At the center is the Seed of Life, ringed by the twelve questions of your beginning, from conception through your first hour: "Am I wanted?", "Am I welcome?", and the rest. They are the same twelve as The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, named here by the need each one asks about, and they are the core first imprint the wheel grows from.

Around them turn seven rights, after Robin Grille's developmental rites of passage, which grow from the body-oriented psychotherapy of Wilhelm Reich and Alexander Lowen: the rights to exist, to need, to have support, to freedom, to love, to playful learning, and to an opinion, a vocation, and sexual autonomy. Each is a stage every child passes through, from the womb to the early twenties. Grille's ages overlap and shift between his own versions, because these are layers laid down over one another, not steps left behind. The first right begins at the top, and the last returns to it.

Reading straight out from the center along any right moves from the child's need, to the imprint and the decision it can leave, to how it can show in you now, to the healing nutrients that restore it, to the belief it can open into. The panel adds three more layers from other sources: gathering the theme across your life, and counteraction and proaction, from William Emerson's somatic work; what tips the scale, from the science of resilience; and why each practice helps, from Candace Pert's work on the chemistry of feeling.

At the very edge, a braid of three strands, the child you were, you now, and the support around you, holds the mandala together.

Not a timeline

Healing rarely goes in order. The same question can begin in the womb, come round again at five or fifteen, and show up today in a relationship or at work. Come in wherever you recognize yourself, and follow the thread in either direction: inward to its earliest root, or outward to what heals it. Emerson found that imprints gather by theme across a life, so that a single moment is rarely the whole story; each right asks you to gather its theme for that reason.

Attachment, and the body

The Attachment tab sets the four adult attachment styles beside the wheel. Attachment research is among the most measured in psychology; the links drawn to particular rights and moments of your beginning are the field's view. The body tab explains implicit memory, the window of tolerance, the ladder of nervous-system states, stress and resilience, and the chemistry of feeling, and how to walk the wheel safely, in small doses.

What is measured, and what is the field's view

This mandala draws on two kinds of sources, and says which is which. Some are measured science with decades behind them: attachment, implicit memory, newborns' recognition of their mother's voice, the window of tolerance as a clinical model, and the science of stress and resilience. Others are frameworks that pre- and perinatal psychology and body-oriented therapy work within: that particular birth events leave particular imprints, that developmental stages carry particular rights, that the body stores emotional memory. These are offered with care as possibilities, worth holding more loosely than the research.

Going gently

This is reflective work, and it can stir strong feelings. Go one ring at a time, and stop whenever you need to. The Pause at the top of the page is always there. You do not need to know the facts of your beginning to begin: many people cannot ask their parents, or should not, and the story you have is enough. Reflection and the practices here are safe to do on your own; regression and deep body work belong with a trained guide, and if what surfaces is heavy, such as trauma, loss, or abuse, do this work alongside a trauma-informed therapist. This mandala is for reflection and education. It is not therapy, and it does not diagnose.

Your own moons

Many traditions count a pregnancy as ten moons. Enter your birthday below the wheel and it counts the full moons of the months you were carried, and names the phase of the moon on the day you were born. With your due date too, each station of Your beginning shows its own dates.

A note on language

Every person and every beginning is its own, and the lines here describe possibilities, not certainties. Your mother here means the person who carried you; if you were adopted or born through surrogacy, that is your birth or gestational mother, and the people who raised you belong to the later rights, which matter just as much. Grille's model is described in our own words; for his full account, see his work.
